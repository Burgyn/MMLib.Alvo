A new booking should charge a deposit, e-mail a confirmation, and remind the customer a day before it starts. Written as
one [event handler](/extending/event-handlers/), a failed e-mail retries the whole handler — and charges the deposit
again. **Steps** fix that: each named step completes once, and a retry returns the stored result of every step that
already completed instead of running it again.

Steps live on the handler's context — `AlvoEventContext.Steps`, and `AlvoJobContext.Steps` for a
[scheduled job](/extending/time/#schedules). They are not a service you resolve: outside a delivery they would have
nothing to remember.

## `RunAsync`: a step completes once

- **Name every step.** A name is 1–200 characters, no control characters, and unique within one run of the handler. It
  is the step's identity across retries, so keep it stable: a renamed step is a new step and runs again.
- **A step is at least once, so its work must be idempotent.** The result is recorded after the work returns; a crash
  between the two runs that one step again. Pass an idempotency key to the payment provider (here, the booking id).
- **A completed step is never run again** for the same event and handler. A retry, a dispatcher restart or another
  replica gets the stored result back.
- **Results are JSON** (`System.Text.Json`, web defaults), at most 256 KiB. Return what the next steps need — a receipt
  id, not the whole response. `RunAsync` without a result stores JSON `null`.
- Two runners racing one step keep the first record, and both continue with its result.

## `SleepUntilAsync`: wait without an attempt

`SleepUntilAsync("day-before", at)` ends the attempt; the delivery goes back to pending, due at `at`, and runs again
then. Completed steps return their stored results and the sleep returns, so the handler continues after it.

- **A sleep spends no attempt.** Five sleeps and a failure still have the retry policy's full retries left.
- **A time already passed returns at once.** A delivery woken early — say, by *run again* — sleeps again.
- **Let it pass.** The sleep ends the attempt by an exception. A handler that catches it still sleeps, and every later
  step of that attempt throws the same exception without running, so each step runs only after the wake.
- **An ordered lane waits.** While a delivery sleeps, later deliveries of the same record for the same handler wait
  behind it, as they wait behind a retry. Sleep in a handler that may hold its lane, or opt out with
  `options.Ordering = AlvoOrdering.None`.
- **For days, not months.** A sleep is the delivery's own due time; long-running workflows that wait for weeks or for
  outside signals belong to a durable-workflow engine ([#374](https://github.com/Burgyn/MMLib.Alvo/issues/374)).

## `Debounce`: one delivery of the latest

`options.Debounce = TimeSpan.FromSeconds(5)` holds each new delivery for five seconds. A newer change of the same record
in that window **supersedes** the waiting one: it ends as `superseded`, unrun, and only the newest runs — with the
record's latest state.

- **Trailing, latest wins**, per handler, record and tenant: changes in two tenants never supersede each other.
- Only a delivery that never started is superseded. One that is running, retrying after a failure, or sleeping runs on.
- 1 second to 1 day. A steady stream of changes faster than the window delays the delivery until it pauses.
- A superseded delivery is finished. It cannot be run again or replayed: the newer one carries the record's state.

## `Concurrency`: cap the running deliveries

`options.Concurrency = AlvoConcurrency.PerKey("tenant", 2)` lets at most two of the handler's deliveries run at once per
tenant, across every replica; `PerKey("record", 1)` at most one per record. The limit is 1–100.

- Per handler: another handler's deliveries never count against it.
- A capped delivery is claimed only together with a free slot. Its answer — success, failure or sleep — frees the slot.
- A crashed runner's slot frees when its claim's lease expires (90 seconds at the defaults).
- Lowering a limit lets deliveries already running finish; the new limit applies to the next claims.

## Running again

*Run again* (redeliver) and *replay* of a **succeeded** delivery delete its steps, so the whole handler runs again. A
**dead** or **cancelled** delivery keeps them: fix the bug, run it again, and it resumes after the last step that
completed. There is no way to force a full re-run of a dead delivery that completed steps — make the steps idempotent and
start a new event if you need one.

## In the dashboard

*Operations* shows a delivery's **Steps** under its attempts — each step's name and when it completed, never its
result, which can hold personal data. A sleeping delivery reads *sleeping until* its wake time; a superseded one reads
*superseded* and offers no *Run again*. A capped handler's row shows its cap, such as *per tenant · 2*.

## Limits

| Thing | Allowed |
|---|---|
| step name | 1–200 characters, no control characters, unique within one run |
| steps per delivery | 1 000 |
| step result | 256 KiB of UTF-8 JSON |
| `Debounce` | 1 second to 1 day |
| `PerKey` limit | 1–100 |
